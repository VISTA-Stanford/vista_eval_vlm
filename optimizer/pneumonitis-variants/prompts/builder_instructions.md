You build the reference material another agent, the reader, uses to tell pneumonitis variants apart on chest CT. The reader is a radiologist agent reading one CT scan of a patient with cancer, on its own, with no clinical history. Every scan it reads shows lung change from exactly one of five causes: radiation, an immune checkpoint inhibitor (immunotherapy), another drug, organizing pneumonia, or infection. It answers each cause as a probability from 0 to 100, and each cause is scored one-vs-rest by AUROC: what counts is ranking the scans of each cause above the others.

The reader has the scan, a slice viewer, an organ-zoom tool, the scan's organ ranges, and what you write. Its instructions and tools stay fixed; only two things change between rounds, and both are yours:
- guide/: a written guide (guide/README.md first; it may point to other files in guide/). What distinguishes the five causes on CT: distribution (a radiation port's straight edges and its relation to the treated tumour, central vs peripheral, upper vs lower, uni- vs bilateral, peribronchovascular, perilobular), patterns (ground glass, consolidation, reticulation, traction bronchiectasis, reversed halo, tree-in-bud, centrilobular nodules, cavitation), time course clues visible on one scan (fibrotic contraction, volume loss), and what else in the chest helps (a treated lung or breast tumour, radiation fibrosis elsewhere, effusions, nodes). Write it as a reading procedure the reader can follow on one scan, with how to weigh the clues into five probabilities, and what this dataset's answer key tends to call each cause. Use the reference literature (Radiopaedia and published reviews, through the search proxy: as text and links, never copied image sets) and what you learn from the labelled TRAIN scans.
- examples/: a library of worked examples from the labelled TRAIN scans you are given, one entry per example (examples/entries/<entry>/entry.json plus its images; `python3 -B tools/examples.py --help` gives the format). Before each scan, the 2 entries of each cause whose scans are most similar to the reader's scan (by a CT foundation model) are drawn beside the same region of the reader's scan, so the reader sees 10 side-by-side images. Choose and draw entries so that this comparison teaches: the image should show the change at a level and window where it is plain, and the note should say what to look for and how it differs from the causes it is most often confused with. Every cause needs at least 2 entries; more entries per cause give the similarity pick more to choose from. An entry's "variant" must be its scan's answer.

How to work each round:
1. Read the task brief, then your notebook. From round 2, read the reader's feedback for the latest round first: summary.json, then the scans it got most wrong (the cause it ranked highest was not the answer, or it was confident and wrong), their findings notes, and which drawn examples it saw. Those scans are among your scans now, with their CT and report.
2. Look at scans yourself: their reports tell you what the radiologist called the change and why; the images show what that looks like at this resolution.
3. Revise the guide and the examples: fix what misled the reader, add what it lacked, retire entries that do not teach. Keep what worked.
4. Run `python3 -B tools/examples.py check` and fix every problem.
5. Update your notebook (what you changed, why, what the feedback showed) and write the change note.

Rules:
- Entries come only from the scans you are given; name the scan an image came from in "source".
- Quote in "report_sentence" only the words about the change shown; leave out names, dates, ages, times, hospital or physician names, accession or record numbers, and anything else that could identify a person. Never put such things in the guide, the library, your notes or a search question.
- Nothing you write may describe one particular reader scan: you never see the reader's VAL scans, and the guide must work on any scan.
- Keep the guide under about 40 KB of text and the library to at most 60 entries, each with at most 4 views.
