You are a radiologist reading one chest CT scan of a patient with cancer. The lungs show new or changed abnormality, and your job is to say what caused it.

How the answer key was made: a language model read the radiologist's report for this scan. Every scan in this set has exactly one of five answers: the report attributes the lung change to radiation (radiation pneumonitis or radiation fibrosis), to an immune checkpoint inhibitor (immunotherapy pneumonitis), to another drug (drug-induced pneumonitis from chemotherapy, targeted therapy or another medicine), to organizing pneumonia (cryptogenic or secondary), or to infection (pneumonia or another lung infection).

QUESTIONS:
1. radiation: Is the lung change radiation pneumonitis or radiation fibrosis?
2. immunotherapy: Is the lung change pneumonitis from an immune checkpoint inhibitor (immunotherapy)?
3. other_drug: Is the lung change drug-induced pneumonitis from a drug other than immunotherapy?
4. cop: Is the lung change organizing pneumonia?
5. infection: Is the lung change an infection (pneumonia)?

Answer each with the probability, from 0 to 100, that the answer is yes. Exactly one of the five is yes for every scan, so give your best estimate for each; they need not add up to 100.

To look at the scan, run `python3 -B tools/view_ct.py --help` (in this program folder) and open the images it writes with the Read tool. Beside the scan's ct/ folder there is organs.json: for this scan, where each organ (the lungs and each lobe, the liver, the heart) lies along the scan's superior-inferior axis, from TotalSegmentator (an automatic organ segmenter): the first, last and centre slice, and the same as fractions of the axis (0 = lowest slice, 1 = highest), which the viewer's --from/--to/--at accept. tools/zoom_organ.py (run `python3 -B tools/zoom_organ.py --help`) uses the scan's organ mask (organ_mask.npz beside ct/) to cut one slice to one lung or lobe and enlarge it, and can enlarge a box inside that crop. Look at both lungs from apex to base, and open at least one zoomed image of each lung before you answer. The distribution of the change (which lobes, central or peripheral, upper or lower, one side or both, its edges), its pattern (ground glass, consolidation, reticulation, nodules, airways) and what else is in the chest (a treated tumour, nodes, effusions) are what separate the five causes.

GUIDE. guide/README.md in this program folder is a guide to telling these five causes apart on CT, written from reference sources and from labelled training scans of other patients. Read it before you look at the scan, and follow what it says; it may point to other files in guide/. If it says it is empty, answer from your own reading.

EXAMPLES DRAWN FOR YOUR SCAN. Beside ct/ there is a folder drawn_examples/: for each of the five causes, the examples in the library (examples/ in this program folder) whose scans are most similar to yours, each already drawn beside the same region of your own scan (left: the example, from another patient; right: your scan; same window and slices). drawn_examples/index.md lists them with each example's cause, the radiologist's words and a note. Read drawn_examples/index.md, then open every image in drawn_examples/ with the Read tool before you answer, and compare the two halves: what does the example show, and is the same thing on your side? An example image you did not open is a missed step. Then look again at your own scan where the comparison points. You may open more library entries with `python3 -B tools/examples.py list` and `python3 -B tools/examples.py open <entry> --scan <your scan's folder>` (the folder that holds ct/ and organs.json). If drawn_examples/ has no images, the library is empty: say so in your findings note and answer from your scan alone. In your findings note, for each example image, write one line: what you saw on each half and whether it changed your reading.
